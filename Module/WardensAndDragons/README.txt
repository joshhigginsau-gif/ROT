Wardens & Dragons  v2.13.2

  A Game of Thrones political layer for a Realm of Thrones campaign, set in
  the years of the Dance of the Dragons. Honour and Dread, wardens and oaths,
  hostages, dragons, the white cloaks, the King's Justice, ravens that may be
  lying, tourneys, hosts bought with gold, the Iron Bank - and at the end of
  every reign, the bastard at the gate.

  This file says what the mod does now. What changed in each version, and
  why, is in CHANGELOG.txt.


WHAT IT NEEDS

  Mount & Blade II: Bannerlord 1.4.8
  Harmony                       required
  Realm of Thrones              the mod is built for its map, its lords and
                                its duels
  Bellum Civile                 optional, but wardens, client realms, the
                                heir choice and the small council all work
                                through it
  RoT Dynasty & Succession      optional; bastards and legitimacy are read
                                the same way by both mods
  War Sails                     optional; hosts get real fleets


INSTALLING AND UPGRADING

  Put the WardensAndDragons folder in your Modules folder and tick it in the
  launcher, below Harmony.

  Upgrading: back up your save, then replace the folder. KEEP YOUR OWN
  config.txt - it lives in bin\Win64_Shipping_Client and is not in the zip.
  Anything a newer version adds is written into it on load, under
  "Added by a newer version". Saves carry over.


HOLDING COURT

  In any town or castle your house holds, the settlement menu offers "Hold
  court" (court_capital_only=true keeps it to your seat). Nearly everything
  in the mod is reached from there:

      House and heirs         your heir, your blade, your culture and style,
                              and your baseborn children
      The small council       while you rule: your council, and hosts
      Wardens and clients     grant realms, set oaths, collect what is owed
      Hostages and wards      the children of other houses at your court
      Realm affairs           your standing, chronicle, Harrenhal, dragons
      Knights of the realm    knight anyone into a house of their own
      The Iron Bank           borrow from Braavos
      The lists               call a tourney
      The King's Justice      hear charges, bring them, answer them
      The white cloaks        the Kingsguard, or your Sworn Shields
      Ravens                  letters, marriages and feasts
      Climb to the Dragonmont at Dragonstone, if your house holds it


HONOUR AND DREAD

  Every ruler carries two numbers, 0 to 100. Honour is what the realm
  believes your word is worth; Dread is how afraid of you it is. Nearly every
  choice in the mod moves one or both, and both drift back toward where they
  started, a little each season.

  They change what others do. Keep your word and lords are likelier to come
  down to single combat; be feared and you are likelier to be sent a false
  raven. And some deeds leave a mark for good: after a false feast, your
  Honour can never again rise as high.


HOUSE AND HEIRS

  NAME YOUR HEIR
    Anyone of your blood, whatever your culture's law says. Bellum Civile
    still shows you the law and who it favours; you are simply allowed to
    break it. The heir you name is the one who takes the seat, even over a
    spouse who married in. Naming one is free; changing a name already given
    costs standing with the realm.

  THE BLADE
    Name your house's ancestral sword, and put it in someone's hand. Whoever
    holds it when you die is the one the realm will say you chose.

  YOUR HOUSE'S CULTURE AND STYLE
    Take up any culture your mods define, for your house and your realm,
    and optionally every holding. Change the style your house wears -
    "Warden of the East" - or wear none.

  BASEBORN CHILDREN
    In a town or village: "Take a room for the night." Three years later
    someone comes to your gate with a child who has your face and a bastard's
    name - Snow, Sand, Waters, Rivers, Storm, Stone, Hill, Flowers, Pyke. Up
    to four in a reign. If you are married, your spouse may hear of it.

    Acknowledge them (House and heirs) and they take your name and a claim,
    at a price with your trueborn kin and the realm. RoT Dynasty reads the
    same thing: while they carry a bastard's name its page says bastard, and
    once they carry yours it says legitimised.


THE BASTARD'S BANNER

  When the ruler of your house dies you are asked, once, whether the one
  who came to the gate during the funeral is who everyone thinks. Say no
  and you are never asked again.

  Say yes and a child of the dead ruler rises against your heir - one you
  had, or, if you never had one, a stranger with your face. What he takes
  depends on what you did for him while you lived:

      ignored him             10% of your sworn houses
      acknowledged him        20%, and one of your holdings
      gave him the blade      33%, a holding, a kingdom of his own, and war
      both                    50%, a holding, a kingdom of his own, and war

  He flies your arms with the colours reversed. Every tourney he won adds
  to his share, and two wins are enough for the crowds to crown him whatever
  you did. Then you choose which of the two you play: stay as your heir, or
  take up his banner.

  EXILE, AND THE COMPANY THAT COMES BACK
    Take every holding his claim has and he does not die - he takes ship to
    Essos, and there founds a sellsword company that grows every season and
    will take anyone's gold but yours. Ten to twenty years later his son
    lands with the whole company, takes a castle and declares war. Beat him
    and the next generation takes ship. It works both ways: take up the
    bastard's banner and win, and it is the old house's heir who sails.


WARDENS, OATHS AND CLIENT REALMS

  As a sovereign, name a house Warden of a realm - in conversation, "Kneel.
  I would name you Warden of a realm" - and swear other houses to it. Every
  house sworn to you, and every client realm, holds you by an oath, set from
  Wardens and clients:

      Exemption     independent in all but name; content, yields nothing
      the Sword     swords instead of gold; they answer every call to war
      Marriage      the steadiest bond; needs a marriage between the houses
      Fealty        the ordinary bond: modest tribute, their host when called
      Tribute       gold instead of swords; heavy dues, slow resentment
      Duress        sworn at swordpoint; they pay a great deal and hate you

  Foreign rulers can be asked to rule as your client (in conversation), on
  any of four arguments. A client realm can later be released, or for
  enough gold and influence brought into your realm outright.


HOSTAGES AND WARDS

  A child of another house lives at your court and does not go home. Call
  them a ward and it is fostering: Honour, warmer relations, and they learn.
  Call them a hostage and it is surety: Dread, and their house keeps faith
  because it must. If it breaks faith anyway, the hostage is forfeit - and
  executing a forfeit hostage costs no Honour. Executing one without cause
  costs a great deal. Sending one home is worth more than never taking one.


HARRENHAL AND THE DRAGONS

  HARRENHAL
    The curse builds on whoever holds Harrenhal, year by year: sleepless
    nights first, and at its height fires and deaths in the family. It fades
    once you let the castle go.

  THE DRAGONS
    The dragons of the Dance are tracked by name: who rides them, which are
    riderless, and which have fallen. Hold Dragonstone and your house may
    send someone up the Dragonmont to claim a riderless dragon - at poor
    odds, and a failed claim usually kills. Cradle eggs may be laid and
    hatch at Dragonstone. Every dragon that dies dims the odds for every egg
    after it.


THE SMALL COUNCIL AND HOSTS  (while you rule)

  SUMMON THE COUNCIL
    Bellum Civile's privy council rides to one of your halls and sits there
    three days. Walk in and speak to them: the Master of Ships musters hosts
    and calls the realm's banners, the Master of Coin raises a levy, the Hand
    tells you how the realm stands, the Master of Laws what waits on the
    King's Justice, the Master of Whisperers what to make of your letters,
    and the Grand Maester the health of your house.

  HOSTS BOUGHT WITH GOLD
    Up to 30,000 men under one of your sworn knights, paid up front for 84
    days: levies at 40 a man, men-at-arms at 80, veterans at 200. They draw
    no wages, carry their own food and do not desert. Order them to besiege,
    hold, march with you, hunt down an enemy host, campaign freely, or stand
    down. A week before their time is up you are asked whether to keep them.

    Every host is raised with a fleet and marches by land or sea as the game
    allows. One sent to besiege or hold that still cannot get there takes
    ship instead - a voyage of 5-12 days - and is put ashore before its
    target.

    Other rulers at war buy hosts too, and answer yours.


PARLEY AT THE WALLS

  Leading a siege with the camp up, the siege menu offers "Ride out under a
  banner of parley". You see both sides' strength, their food and how long
  they have held. Then:

      Single combat     the one easy road. Whether they come down depends on
                        the courage of whoever fights for the walls. Realm of
                        Thrones' own duel, on the field. Win and the castle
                        yields - usually. Lose and you swear off the siege.
      Terms             hard: they have to be starving, or besieged for
                        weeks, or hopelessly outnumbered
      Gold              in the millions
      Talk them round   three arguments, and every one must land

  When the gates open under your word you choose: keep it, or seize the
  lords as they march out.


KNIGHTS OF THE REALM  (as a ruler)

  Knight a soldier of your host, a companion, or a wanderer in the town you
  are in. For 15,000 they are styled Ser and found a house with a name you
  choose, a banner and no land, which rides for your realm as a free
  company. Like sellswords they go when they please once the first contract
  is up - and may take service with your enemies.


THE IRON BANK

  Braavos lends by your house's tier, holdings and renown, and by what it
  thinks of you: 10-30% interest, repaid in 4, 8 or 12 payments. Miss one
  and you get a letter, and a tenth more to pay. Miss two and the Bank
  starts paying your strongest enemy to hunt you. Miss three and you are in
  default. Rulers borrow too, and if one at war with you cannot pay, the
  Bank may offer you a cheap loan to finish them.


THE LISTS

  Call a tourney in a town your house holds, once a year: modest, great or
  lavish. Guest houses bring their young lords, and you choose who of your
  blood rides - your baseborn children included, which is the dangerous
  part. Lords die in the lists now and then. Win, and you choose whose lap
  the roses land in.


THE KING'S JUSTICE

  Treason, murder, kinslaying and the killing of captives are written down
  as they happen. Hear a charge against any lord of your realm or anyone you
  hold, or buy the witnesses for one. The accused may demand trial by
  combat, or for grave charges a trial of seven, fought for real in a
  town's arena if you stand in it yourself. Anyone who falls in a trial may
  never rise, you included.
  And the charge can come to you.


THE WHITE CLOAKS

  Seven knights sworn for life: the Kingsguard (or Queensguard) if you rule
  a realm, your house's Sworn Shields if not. They walk with you through
  towns and your hall in the white armour, stand first as your champions,
  clear outlaw camps and hunt fugitives. A knight who marries or leaves
  your house is an oathbreaker.


RAVENS

  About a letter a month from other houses: a match, a feast, word from
  your kin, a feast to end a war. Most mean what they say. Some do not. Your
  people tell you what they make of each, and are right more often the more
  Roguery you have and the more white cloaks ride with you. Go to a false
  feast and the doors close behind you - in your feast clothes, unless you
  came armed.

  You can send ravens too: offer a marriage, hold an honest feast - or lay
  a feast they will not leave.


SETTINGS

  config.txt, in bin\Win64_Shipping_Client, holds every number the mod uses,
  each with a line saying what it does, and most systems can be switched
  off there.
  The file is written on first run and topped up by newer versions.


KNOWN LIMITS

  A host hunting an enemy across the sea is not ferried; only siege and
  hold orders are. Armies called to the banners by the council choose sea
  routes where the game allows, but are not ferried either.


TESTING AND THE LOG

  Open the console (Alt + ~) and type wad.cheats for every test command.
  Save first: they run the real thing.

  The mod writes wardens_dragons.log next to its DLL. It records every
  decision that matters, so a log is the fastest way to report a problem.
